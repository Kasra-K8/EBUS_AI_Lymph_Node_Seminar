# AI and Deep Learning for EBUS Lymph Node Analysis

## Master's Seminar  Biomedical Engineering

This repository contains my M.Sc. seminar presentation on the use of **Endobronchial Ultrasound (EBUS)**, medical image analysis, and artificial intelligence for the assessment of malignant lymph nodes in lung cancer.

The seminar was completed as part of the **Master's Seminar (سمینار ارشد)** course in Biomedical Engineering at **K. N. Toosi University of Technology**.

The work reviews conventional EBUS-based lymph-node assessment methods and examines the growing role of **deep learning, convolutional neural networks, image segmentation, multimodal imaging, and artificial intelligence** in automated EBUS analysis.

> 🏆 **Award:** This seminar was selected as the **Best Biomedical Engineering Seminar at K. N. Toosi University of Technology** and was also recognized among the university's outstanding seminar presentations.

## Overview

Endobronchial Ultrasound-Guided Transbronchial Needle Aspiration (**EBUS-TBNA**) is a minimally invasive technique used to examine and sample mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes.

This seminar investigates how information obtained from EBUS imaging can be used for lymph-node malignancy assessment and reviews how artificial intelligence can assist or automate different stages of this process.

The presentation progresses from conventional diagnostic approaches to modern deep-learning-based methods for:

- Lymph-node malignancy classification
- Lymph-node segmentation
- Blood-vessel segmentation
- Analysis of EBUS elastography
- Multimodal EBUS analysis
- Lymph-node station identification
- Cytological image analysis
- Prediction of lymph-node metastasis

---

## Main Topics

### 1. EBUS-TBNA

The seminar first introduces the EBUS-TBNA procedure, including:

- Endobronchial ultrasound imaging
- Ultrasound-equipped bronchoscopy
- Selection of lymph nodes for sampling
- Needle-based tissue sampling
- Ultrasound guidance during the procedure

---

### 2. Comparison with Conventional Diagnostic Methods

The presentation reviews EBUS in relation to other diagnostic approaches, including:

- PET-CT
- Contrast-enhanced CT
- EBUS-FNA
- EBUS-TBNA

The advantages and diagnostic capabilities of EBUS-based approaches for mediastinal lymph-node assessment are discussed.

---

### 3. Sonographic Scoring Systems

Several studies using conventional EBUS sonographic characteristics for predicting lymph-node malignancy are reviewed.

Features discussed include:

- Lymph-node size
- Shape
- Margins
- Echogenicity / echotexture
- Central hilar structure
- Necrosis
- Vascularity
- Elastography characteristics

These approaches demonstrate how combinations of sonographic features can be used to estimate the probability of malignancy before or alongside pathological confirmation.

## Artificial Intelligence and Deep Learning

A major part of the seminar focuses on research investigating deep-learning approaches for EBUS analysis.

Multiple architectures and applications were reviewed, including:

- **SqueezeNet**
- **U-Net**
- **MobileNet**
- **VGG-based CNNs**
- **NeuralSeg**
- **E-Net**
- **EBUS-Net**
- **Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN)**
- Other convolutional neural network architectures

## Deep Learning Applications Reviewed

### Lymph Node Classification

CNN-based methods for distinguishing malignant and benign lymph nodes from EBUS images were reviewed, including models trained using frames extracted from EBUS videos.

---

### Lymph Node and Blood Vessel Segmentation

U-Net-based approaches were examined for automatic segmentation of mediastinal lymph nodes and surrounding blood vessels.

Automatic segmentation may reduce operator dependence and provide a foundation for computer-assisted EBUS analysis.

---

### Explainable AI

Several reviewed approaches incorporated visualization techniques such as **Grad-CAM** to identify image regions contributing to model predictions.

Explainability is particularly important in medical AI because it can provide additional insight into how a neural network reaches a diagnostic prediction.

---

### EBUS Elastography

The seminar reviews AI-assisted EBUS elastography methods for evaluating tissue stiffness and distinguishing malignant from benign lymph nodes.

These approaches combine quantitative elastography information with automated image-analysis methods.

---

### Multimodal EBUS Analysis

Deep-learning systems combining multiple EBUS imaging modalities were also examined.

Multimodal approaches can integrate complementary information from different ultrasound modes to improve lymph-node assessment.

---

### Lymph Node Station Identification

The seminar also reviews deep-learning methods designed to automatically identify thoracic lymph-node stations during EBUS procedures.

Such systems may support real-time navigation and assist less experienced operators during bronchoscopy.

---

### Cytology and Whole-Slide Image Analysis

AI methods applied to EBUS-TBNA cytology and digital whole-slide images were also reviewed.

These systems aim to automatically identify malignant regions within cytological samples and potentially assist pathological assessment.

---

## Research Perspective

This seminar formed an important foundation for my subsequent research in medical image analysis.

Reviewing the progression from conventional EBUS sonographic characteristics to deep-learning-based classification and segmentation motivated my interest in developing AI-based methods for the automatic assessment of lymph-node malignancy from EBUS images.

My subsequent research has focused on deep learning, medical image analysis, and computer-aided diagnosis in lung cancer.
